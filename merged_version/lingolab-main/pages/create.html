Start by considering your sound profile.

Ever wondered why languages sound so different?

Every language has a collection of speech sounds, called phonemes, that make up every word in that language. They can be vowels, consonants, or even clicking. These unique Sound Profiles give each language its distinct rhythm & flavor.

Did You Know?

The language with the most phonemes is called Taa. It has over 140 unique sounds & is spoken by a small group of people in Botswana & Namibia.

The language with the least phonemes has only 11. It’s called Rotokas, & is spoken on an island in Papua New Guinea.

Explore some phonemes & think about what type of sound you want your language to have. A language with lots of “p”, “k”, & “t” sounds is going to have a very different vibe from a language that utilizes a lot of “f”, “m”, & “r” sounds.

What about languages that don’t have sound?

Just like spoken languages have phonemes as building blocks for their words, signed languages have building blocks that make up signs. These are called parameters. American Sign Language (ASL) has five parameters that work together to define each sign.

Handshape: What shape is each hand making?

Palm Orientation: What direction is each palm facing?

Location: Where does each hand begin?

Movement: How does each hand move?

Non-Manual Signals: ASL isn’t just hands. Many signs rely on facial expressions as well, which fall under this parameter.
