Three classroom arcs

Lesson plans with a clear next move.

Each plan can stand alone or become part of a sequence. Students leave with something they can explain, test, or revise.

0140 min

Build a language profile

Students make a claim about how their language works, then support it with examples.

GoalIdentify patterns in word order, tense, and negation.
MaterialsLingoLab create flow, board, sticky notes.
  1. Notice · 7 min Compare two translated sentences and circle what changes.
  2. Make · 18 min Students configure one feature in their language.
  3. Explain · 10 min Partners predict a new sentence and justify the prediction.
  4. Exit · 5 min Write one rule using “When…, my language…”.
0245 min

Translation detectives

Students translate, compare, and debug sentences where a small grammatical choice changes the meaning.

GoalUse evidence to distinguish tense, number, articles, and negation.
MaterialsTranslation challenge, notebooks, “because” sentence stems.
  1. Warm up · 5 min Predict which sentence will be hardest and why.
  2. Play · 15 min Complete the six-round challenge independently.
  3. Repair · 15 min Revise one missed answer and name the broken rule.
  4. Share · 10 min Defend one translation to the class.
0335 min

When words travel

Students explore how meaning shifts when a phrase has no neat one-word equivalent.

GoalSeparate literal translation from meaning-making and context.
MaterialsExplore page, word cards, comparison sheet.
  1. Encounter · 8 min Find a phrase that needs more than one English word.
  2. Remix · 12 min Create two context-specific translations.
  3. Test · 10 min Swap with a partner and infer the intended meaning.
  4. Reflect · 5 min Name what was lost, added, or clarified.
Fast activities

Drop-in moves for any class.

Use one as a starter, transition, or five-minute finish.

5 minutes · Starter

Spot the difference

Display two translations that differ by one feature. Students mark the changed word or affix, then complete: “This probably means ___ because ___.”

8 minutes · Pair work

Invent the error

One student writes a convincing but incorrect translation. Their partner locates the error and cites the language rule that fixes it.

5 minutes · Exit ticket

One rule, one question

Students submit one rule they can explain and one question they still have about how meaning is encoded.

Assessment lens

Listen for reasoning, not just accuracy.

  • Emerging: identifies a changed word or ending.
  • Developing: connects the change to a pattern.
  • Secure: predicts a new translation and explains the evidence.
Teacher prompt

“What makes you think that?”

Keep this question visible during challenge play. It shifts the room from guessing answers to making testable claims about language.
